Distorted Democracies

Pablo Beramendi, Carles Boix, Marc Guinjoan, Melissa Rogers

AggregateCross-countryContemporary

To evaluate the extent of malapportionment, that is, the discrepancy between the relative allocation of seats and the relative allocation of votes across districts, we compile and examine a new, comprehensive dataset that includes population and allocation of seats by district and partisan ideology in over 20,000 districts and 1,100 different parties across 65 democracies during the period 1996-2020 in lower and upper chambers. In addition, we pool Spanish survey data (N=488,000) to examine directly how voters' preferences translate into representation as mediated by malapportionment. Our evidence shows that malapportionment is larger in upper than in lower chambers. Contrary to established views, we find that malapportionment is ideologically neutral in the vast majority of lower house elections, and where a detectable bias exists, it is as likely to favor the left as the right. Upper houses present a sharper asymmetry: a rightward bias predominates, and among cases of strong bias, it is exclusively rightward.

Electoral institutions, malapportionment and the representation of ethnic minorities

Marc Guinjoan, Jordi Mas, Xavier Roura

Territory, Politics and Governance (forthcoming)

AggregateCross-countryContemporary

The representation of ethnic minorities in national legislatures varies widely: in some cases minorities are privileged through ethnic reserved seats or territorial concentration, while in others they remain systematically excluded. This paper addresses a gap in the literature by introducing a novel cross-national dataset covering more than 22,000 legislative districts in 84 countries (1996–2020) in lower-house elections, which for the first time identifies, at the district level, the presence of ethnic minorities excluded from executive power. Our empirical analyses show that countries with ethnic reserved seats for minorities tend to exhibit higher malapportionment, and that minority-concentrated districts are more often over-represented. We also find that over-representation is most pronounced in open autocracies and weak democracies, a pattern consistent with the strategic use of electoral rules.

Unpacking Representation Bias: A Simulation Approach to Electoral Institutions

Marc Guinjoan, David Carbonell, Melissa Rogers

AggregateCross-countryContemporary

This paper develops a simulation-based framework to decompose ideological representation bias — the gap between the ideological center of the electorate and that of the elected legislature — into the distinct institutional sources that produce it. We simulate large numbers of synthetic polities (100 districts, up to 10,000 runs per exercise) in which four institutional parameters — malapportionment, district magnitude, the seat-allocation formula, and the legal electoral threshold — are introduced exogenously and independently of each other, and validate the decomposition against real electoral data from Spain's 2019 European Parliament election. Results show that malapportionment bias is generally modest under random territorial imbalance but can become substantial when ideologically concentrated; that seat representation bias is consistently the dominant source of distortion; and that legal thresholds generate meaningful bias only well above common European practice. We accompany the paper with REPBIAS, an open-source Shiny application for research and classroom use.

Strategic Distortions: Electoral Reforms in Authoritarian Regimes

Jaime Bordel

AggregateCross-countryContemporary

The literature on electoral reforms in authoritarian regimes has gained increasing relevance in recent years. However, two fundamental questions remain unresolved: when do autocrats reform electoral rules, and why do they adopt some types of reforms over others? This paper argues that electoral reforms are strategic responses to bottom-up pressures rather than signs of liberalization or regime strength, and that the salience of ethnic cleavages conditions the content of reform. Using an original dataset covering all electoral reforms in electoral autocracies from 1980 to 2023, the empirical evidence shows that authoritarian control problems increase the likelihood of reform, while ethnic fractionalization significantly raises the probability of adopting partisan-biased reforms.

How Tailored Information Influences Election Dynamics

Marc Guinjoan, Nikandros Ioannidis, Toni Rodon, Fernando Mendez, Costas Djoivas

IndividualSpainContemporary

Voting Advice Applications (VAAs) have become increasingly popular tools for delivering personalized political information to voters ahead of elections. Using an experimental design deployed in Spain during the 2023 national election (N=156,403) — the largest VAA experiment ever fielded — we identify the causal effect of party information provision on voters' likelihood of supporting a given party. Users exposed to personalized information reported significantly higher probabilities of voting for the party they already intended to support, and a lower vote intention for other parties within the same ideological bloc; the effect was strongest among undecided voters. Overall, the evidence suggests that VAAs may reinforce existing predispositions to vote for a preselected party, but we find no indication that they lead to vote switching.

Beyond Partisanship: Territorial and Negative Self-Interest in Electoral System Evaluations

Jaime Bordel

IndividualSpainContemporary

Research on how citizens evaluate electoral rules typically contrasts explanations based on normative principles with those centered on partisan self-interest. This paper develops a self-interest-based framework showing that self-interest can operate along multiple political cleavages beyond partisanship, incorporating territorial self-interest, and that negative information about how rules treat disliked actors can be especially mobilizing. A vignette survey experiment in Spain (n = 3,010) shows that partisan and territorial self-interest shape citizens' evaluations in uneven ways across ideological blocks: treatments signaling that partisan actors were disadvantaged reduced satisfaction among both left- and right-wing voters, while treatments activating territorial conflict produced larger shifts among right-wing respondents.

Legacies of Caciquism on Electoral Coordination

Jaime Bordel-Gil, Marc Guinjoan, Toni Rodon, Pau Vall-Prat

AggregateSpainHistorical

Early draft — abstract not yet available. Examines how exposure to caciquismo — clientelist electoral control — during the Spanish Restoration shaped electoral coordination decades later, during the Second Republic (1931–1936). Using a regression discontinuity design around historical district borders, the paper shows that municipalities more exposed to clientelist control display a higher share of wasted votes once competitive elections return, consistent with the erosion of the civic and partisan structures voters rely on to coordinate their choices.
